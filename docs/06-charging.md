# 6. Charging and batteries

Batteries are the most expensive consumable on the robot and the easiest thing
to degrade through inattention. This page is short. Please read it anyway.

## What you have

- **2 batteries**
- **1 battery charger**, with power cable, Spot charging cable, and battery
  charging cable

Two batteries means one can charge while the other is in use. A full charge
takes roughly 2 hours; a battery gives roughly 90 minutes of walking, less with
payloads drawing power.

## The rules that matter

| | |
|---|---|
| **Don't run flat** | Land and swap well before 10%. Deep discharge shortens battery life permanently |
| **Don't store flat** | If a battery won't be used for days, leave it part-charged, not empty |
| **Charge before return** | Please hand both batteries back charged |
| **Let it cool** | A hot battery straight off the robot charges poorly. Give it a few minutes |
| **Don't leave it charging unattended overnight** | Standard lithium practice |

## Two ways to charge

1. **Battery out of the robot**, on the charger with the battery charging
   cable. This is the normal approach and lets you keep operating on the other
   battery.
2. **Robot plugged in directly**, using the Spot charging cable. Convenient,
   but the robot is out of action while it charges.

## Swapping a battery

1. Sit the robot and power it down fully.
2. Release the latch and lift the battery out from the top.
3. Slide the fresh battery in until it clicks.
4. Power on.

Don't hot-swap. Power the robot down first.

## If a battery misbehaves

Swelling, unusual heat, physical damage, or a refusal to charge: stop using it
immediately, put it somewhere non-flammable, and contact us. Don't try to
revive it.
